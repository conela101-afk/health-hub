**Unclear:** no single general HSE patient page on how to request an interpreter; "On Speaking Terms" (2009) is hosted only on lenus.ie and was quoted from third-party pages, so do not use those quotes.

**Draft:** If English is not your first language, or you use Irish Sign Language, tell the service before your appointment that you need an interpreter. HSE guidance for staff says a professional interpreter should be used, and that relying on family or children to interpret is not recommended. Under the Irish Sign Language Act 2017, public bodies must do all that is reasonable to provide ISL interpretation free of charge when you use their services.

**Gaps:** HSE patient-facing general interpreter page; NI interpreting (HSC Regional Interpreting Service) not researched.

**Staff-facing source (staff guidance):** the HSE National Guidelines on Accessible Health and Social Care Services (https://assets.publications.hse.ie/media/file_based_publications/natguideaccessibleservices.pdf) is staff guidance.
